NBA Players Data Exploration

SQL Data Exploration Project

12,844 player records · 27 seasons · 1996-97 to 2022-23

Exploring NBA player data through SQL, with a focus on season leaders, draft position, college background, and international growth.

Project Goal

The goal of this project was to demonstrate practical SQL skills by exploring NBA player data and answering structured analytical questions. The analysis focused on season leaders, draft position, college background, and the long-term growth of international representation in the NBA across 27 seasons, from 1996-97 through 2022-23.

Technologies & Tools Used

SQL, Microsoft Excel, PostgreSQL


SQL Skills:

  1. Data filtering with WHERE
  2. Aggregation with COUNT(), AVG(), MIN(), and MAX()
  3. Grouping and aggregated filtering with GROUP BY and HAVING
  4. Common Table Expressions
  5. Combining query results with LEFT JOIN and CROSS JOIN
  6. Window functions using RANK() and PARTITION BY
  7. Percentage calculations and player rankings
  8. Player-level identifier creation using DENSE_RANK()

Step by Step:

  1. Imported the source CSV file into a PostgreSQL table containing 12,844 player records across 27 NBA seasons.
  2. Used the original CSV index as the primary key for individual records.
  3. Created a player-level identifier with DENSE_RANK() based on player name, college, and draft year.
  4. Explored the dataset by writing initial SQL queries to understand its structure, available fields, season range, and player records.
  5. Defined analytical questions focused on season leaders, draft position, college background, and international representation.
  6. Wrote and organized SQL queries to answer each analytical question and identify meaningful patterns in the data.
  7. Reviewed the query results and selected the most relevant findings for the final analysis.
  8. Exported the SQL queries and selected results into separate files for clear project documentation.
  9. Created a summary of the analysis, documented the project in a README, and prepared the final materials for GitHub and the portfolio website.

SQL Analysis Overview

The final project was organized into four analytical areas:

Dataset Overview - dataset size, available seasons, and changes in the number of player records over time.

Season Leaders - players with the highest combined points, rebounds, and assists per game in each season.

Draft and College Analysis - scoring performance by draft position, second-round and undrafted players, and college background.

International Players - international representation by season, leading countries, and the highest-scoring international players.

Insights / Findings

    1. International representation increased substantially.

      International players represented 2.04% of player records in 1996-97 and 23.38% in 2022-23. The increase occurred across most of the analyzed period, indicating a clear long-term shift toward a more international league.

    2. Canada was the largest source of international players.

      Canada contributed 48 unique players, followed by France with 37 and Australia with 31.

    3. International growth was accompanied by strong individual performance.

      Luka Doncic recorded the highest average scoring result among international players at 27.70 points per game, followed by Joel Embiid at 26.54.

    4. The first overall draft pick produced the highest scoring average among first-round positions.

      Players selected first averaged 16.46 points per game. However, scoring averages did not decline consistently with each subsequent draft position.

    5. Strong scorers were also found outside the first round.

      Nikola Jokic, selected with the 41st pick, averaged 20.40 points per game. Several second-round and undrafted players also appeared among the leading scorers in these groups.

    6. College scoring averages and player representation showed different patterns.

      Oklahoma and Wake Forest recorded the highest scoring averages among colleges represented by at least 10 players. Kentucky and Duke combined strong averages with substantially larger groups of players.

    7. Russell Westbrook recorded the highest season-leading combined average.

      The combined points, rebounds, and assists average reached 52.7 per game in the 2016-17 season.

Analytical Value

  • Supports historical analysis of changes in expedition activity and team size
  • Identifies peaks combining high popularity with strong expedition success
  • Separates absolute deaths from relative risk to prevent misleading comparisons
  • Demonstrates how minimum observation thresholds improve the reliability of success-rate rankings
  • Enables geographic exploration of participation and performance by citizenship
  • Provides an interactive foundation for comparing years, seasons, regions, height bands, peaks, and countries

Data Limitations

Data for 2024 ends in June and represents a partial year.
Participant records represent person-expedition entries, not unique climbers.
Detailed hired-personnel records may be incomplete.
Records with missing, uncertain, or multiple citizenship values are not displayed on the map.
The results identify patterns and relationships but do not establish their causes.
