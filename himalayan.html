Stripe Project

Sales Analytics Dashboard – Stripe Transactions Analysis

Project Goal

The dashboard was created to analyze additional sales generated through the Spacebring application. The project focuses on identifying key sales patterns, analyzing commissions, and supporting operational optimization based on 17 months of transaction data from the Stripe platform.

Technologies and Tools Used

MS Excel, Power Pivot, Power Query, Macros, DAX

Step by Step:

  • Data preparation and cleaning in Power Query, split into four tables (fact and dimension).
  • Building a data model in Power Pivot (snowflake schema) and organizing table relationships.
  • Creating a dedicated measure container and implementing 17 measures in DAX.
  • Preparing the dashboard workspace – creating pivot tables as the foundation for interactive visualizations.
  • Designing the dashboard – building visualizations and adding custom slicers for intuitive data exploration and filtering.
  • Creating two macros and assigning them to buttons:
    • Macro 1 – filtering the dashboard to present an interesting analytical insight.
    Macro 2 – clearing all filters and restoring the initial view.
  • Final dashboard exploration, improving design and aesthetics, drawing conclusions, and preparing documentation.

Final Dashboard Overview

Insights / Findings

  • A single client generated over 60% of revenue in the two best-performing months (May and September 2024), indicating a high revenue concentration and potential dependency risk on one client.
  • Sales are mostly concentrated in the middle of the week (Tuesday – Wednesday). The exception is Monday, which shows high values due to one key client’s transactions (see point 1).
  • The return rate remains low at 2.30%, and in some months drops to 0%, suggesting high customer satisfaction.
  • No repeatable sales patterns were observed in early-year data (e.g., March 2024 – PLN 3,653 vs March 2025 – PLN 6,740), making forecasting more challenging.
  • The number of transactions strongly correlates with sales (similar trend lines), confirming that revenue growth is mainly driven by a higher number of orders rather than changes in average order value.

Potential Business Use Cases

  • Valuable for sales and operations teams to analyze front desk efficiency in generating additional revenue.
  • Supports operational optimization decisions – e.g., reducing reception staffing on weaker sales days (Fridays) could lower costs without impacting revenue.
  • Helps identify key clients generating the majority of revenue, enabling dedicated service and loyalty programs.
  • Comparing additional sales with other revenue streams.
  • Pricing strategy – analyzing the average order value can support effective and dynamic pricing decisions.
